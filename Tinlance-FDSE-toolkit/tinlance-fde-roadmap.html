01 — Foundation

What forward deployed engineering actually is

FDE is not consulting. It is not freelancing. It is embedding your engineering capability inside a client's team and owning outcomes — not just deliverables.

⚡

You are the bridge

Between a client's business problem and a working technical solution. You translate messy requirements into deployed systems — fast.

🔁

You own outcomes

Not just code. An FDE is accountable for whether the thing works in production, whether the team can maintain it, and whether it moves the metric.

🚀

Speed is the product

FDEs build PoCs in 48 hours, full integrations in days. The value is that you move at a pace internal teams cannot match.

🧠

Domain expertise is the moat

Generic developers are replaceable. An FDE who understands AI deployment or cybersecurity deeply commands a premium no generalist can touch.

FDE (what you are)
  • Embeds inside the client's environment
  • Owns the outcome, not just the ticket
  • Builds fast, iterates in real time
  • Speaks both business and engineering
  • Brings proprietary tools (ThreatFade, etc.)
  • Retains IP unless contract says otherwise
vs
Consultant / Freelancer
  • Stays at arm's length, gives advice
  • Delivers a document or a repo and leaves
  • Works to a fixed scope and timeline
  • Speaks mostly to project managers
  • Uses generic tools and frameworks
  • Usually transfers IP by default

05 — Sales motion

How to find and close your first clients

FDE clients don't browse Fiverr. They come through credibility signals, warm networks, and targeted outreach. Here is the exact sequence to follow.

01
Build your public proof
Before any outreach, you need evidence. Clients hiring an FDE are making a high-trust decision. Your open-source PRs, ThreatFade production results, and BugFlow deployment are your proof points — make them findable.
Action: Write one technical case study for each of your three main products. Publish on tinlance.com/work and LinkedIn. Title format: "How we [outcome] for [client type] in [timeframe]." Use your own products as the client if you have no external clients yet.
02
Target the right buyers
FDE buyers are CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and Heads of Security at companies with 20–500 employees. They have budget, they feel the pain, and they can make decisions without committee approval.
Verticals to start with: Nigerian fintech (Paystack ecosystem, Flutterwave partners), European scale-ups needing NIS2/DORA compliance (your KalevioAI research is directly useful here), and AU/APAC companies in the NG↔AU BEC fraud corridor — you literally built ReconOS for this exact market.
03
Outreach that works
Cold email works if it leads with their problem, not your capabilities. One sentence on who you are, two sentences on the specific problem you solve for their type of company, one sentence on what you want.
Template: "Hi [Name] — I'm Chinaemerem, founder of Tinlance. We help [fintech / SaaS / enterprise] companies deploy [AI capabilities / security infrastructure] in weeks, not quarters. We recently [specific outcome]. Worth a 20-minute call to see if it's relevant?" — nothing more.
04
Run the discovery call right
The discovery call is not a sales pitch. It is a diagnostic. Ask about their current state, their desired state, what has blocked them, and what success looks like in 90 days. Talk for no more than 30% of the call.
End every discovery call with: "Based on what you've told me, I think a [Sprint / Embed / Retainer] is the right fit. I'll send you a one-page scope within 48 hours. Does that work?" — never leave the call without a concrete next step.
05
Close with a scoped proposal
One page. Problem statement (their words), proposed solution, deliverables, timeline, price, payment terms, what you need from them. No 20-page PDFs — they don't get read.
Use a Notion page or a clean PDF. Include a "What this is not" section — it builds trust and prevents scope creep before it starts. Add a 5-day acceptance window to create urgency without pressure.
06
Deliver, then expand
The best FDE clients come from referrals. Every engagement should end with: a clean handoff, a documented outcome, and a direct ask for a referral or testimonial. The second engagement is always easier to close than the first.
After every delivery: send a one-paragraph outcome summary to the client. Then ask: "Is there anyone else in your network who might benefit from this?" — most founders never ask. The ones who do fill their pipeline without paid ads.
